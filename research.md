# Research: Home Inventory Manager

**Date:** 2026-05-02
**Status:** Candidate research

---

## 1. Problem Statement

Most people have no systematic record of their possessions. When disaster strikes — house fire, flood, theft, or natural disaster — insurance claims are hobbled by the inability to recall what was owned, provide purchase prices, or supply serial numbers. The same gap affects estate planning (heirs cannot identify or value assets), moving (items are packed without a record), and downsizing (value is lost when items are discarded unknowingly). A home inventory manager that makes documentation easy to create and maintain — and that stores photos, receipts, and valuations in a secure, accessible location — solves a problem people know they have but consistently defer.

## 2. Market Landscape

The home inventory app space is relatively niche but practically important, driven by insurance industry advocacy and the growing frequency of natural disasters and property crimes. Several established apps exist, but most focus narrowly on insurance documentation rather than broader lifecycle use cases (moving, estate planning, downsizing).

Key platforms in this space include:

- **HomeZada** — comprehensive home management platform with a home inventory module covering photos, receipts, warranties, and insurance documentation. ([homezada.com](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/home-inventory))
- **Under My Roof** (iOS/Mac) — stores photos, receipts, product manuals, warranties, notes, and important documents; includes insurance-specific tools and property and maintenance logs. ([everspruceapp.com](https://everspruceapp.com/best-home-inventory-app-for-insurance/))
- **Encircle** — free app covering insurance purposes, estate planning, relocation, and home decoration; targets multiple use cases beyond claims. ([sagesure.com](https://sagesure.com/insurance-insights/home-inventory-apps-to-document-your-personal-property/))
- **NAIC Home Inventory App** — free app from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners enabling manual entry and barcode scanning for insurance documentation. ([wsrinsurance.com](https://www.wsrinsurance.com/home-inventory-apps-can-help-document-your-personal-property/))
- **SaveOr** — modern home inventory platform explicitly built for downsizing, estate planning, moving, and insurance; captures the stories, history, and personal significance behind items alongside financial value, going beyond pure documentation. ([saveor.com](https://www.saveor.com/home-inventory-apps-guide))
- **Artifcts** — focuses on the sentimental and provenance value of objects alongside their financial worth, targeting estate planning and family legacy use cases. ([artifcts.com](https://artifcts.com/articles/post/how-to-choose-the-right-home-inventory-app-for-you))

## 3. Key Features to Consider

- **Item cataloguing** — recording item name, description, category, location in the home, purchase date, purchase price, and current estimated value
- **Photo and receipt capture** — attaching multiple photos per item alongside digital receipts, warranties, manuals, and serial number records
- **Barcode and serial number scanning** — using the device camera to capture barcodes and serial numbers for accurate identification and manufacturer lookup
- **Room and location organisation** — structuring inventory by room, building, or storage location for easy navigation and export
- **Insurance export** — generating formatted inventory reports (PDF, spreadsheet) structured for insurance claim submission, with item totals and supporting evidence
- **Value tracking and depreciation** — estimated replacement value versus actual cash value; tracking how item values change over time
- **Cloud backup and sharing** — secure offsite backup (critical if the home and all physical records are destroyed); controlled sharing with insurers, family members, or estate attorneys
- **Estate planning integration** — tagging items with intended beneficiaries, provenance notes, and sentimental context for inheritance purposes

---

## 4. Technical Considerations

- **Photo management** — handling large volumes of item photos efficiently; compression and deduplication to manage storage costs; image search or AI tagging to find items without manual browsing
- **Offline-first operation** — cataloguing continues when moving or in areas with poor connectivity; background sync when online
- **Cross-platform availability** — iOS and Android for room-by-room cataloguing on mobile; web or desktop for bulk data management and report generation
- **AI-assisted categorisation** — using computer vision to identify item type from a photo and pre-populate fields, reducing manual entry friction
- **Data portability** — export in open formats (CSV, PDF) so users are not locked in and can share with insurers or family members regardless of software
- **Security and access control** — end-to-end encryption for sensitive financial and personal data; multi-factor authentication; role-based sharing (read-only access for estate attorneys or insurers)
- **Valuation data integration** — pulling market value estimates from resale platforms or appraisal databases for high-value items (electronics, jewellery, art, collectibles)

---

## 5. Differentiation Opportunities

- **AI item recognition from photos** — pointing a camera at a room and having the system identify and propose catalogue entries for visible items dramatically lowers the activation energy for first-time cataloguing
- **Replacement value estimator** — automatically suggesting current replacement cost using retail price databases, updated periodically so the inventory reflects current market prices rather than stale purchase prices
- **Moving mode** — generating room-by-room packing lists from the inventory, tracking which boxes items are in, and producing an arrival checklist — repurposing the existing item database for a key life moment
- **Estate planning workflow** — guided flow for assigning items to beneficiaries, recording provenance and sentimental notes, and generating a formatted personal property memorandum for use in estate documents
- **Insurance integration partnerships** — working directly with insurers to allow one-click inventory submission at policy inception or claim time, creating a distribution channel and network effect
